## Skill packs

A pack practises part of what an expectation asks; the note under each mapping says which part. Mapped expectations still link to the full official text.

- `frac-simplify` — Simplifying fractions
  - Grade 7 B1.4: Practises using equivalent fractions to write a fraction in simplest form.
- `frac-addsub` — Adding and subtracting fractions
  - Grade 6 B2.5: Adds and subtracts fractions with like and unlike denominators. Tools such as fraction strips appear in explanations; their use is not assessed.
  - Grade 7 B2.5: Adds and subtracts fractions, including by creating equivalent fractions.
  - Grade 8 B2.5: Adds and subtracts fractions, including mixed numbers and negative results.
- `frac-muldiv` — Multiplying and dividing fractions
  - Grade 6 B2.9: Multiplies whole numbers by proper fractions.
  - Grade 6 B2.10: Divides whole numbers by proper fractions.
  - Grade 7 B2.8: Multiplies and divides fractions by fractions. Tools are shown in explanations, not assessed.
  - Grade 8 B2.6: Multiplies and divides fractions by fractions, whole numbers and mixed numbers.
- `fdp-convert` — Fractions, decimals and percents
  - Grade 7 B1.7: Converts between fractions, decimal numbers and percents.
  - Grade 7 B2.2: Practises recall of commonly used percent, fraction and decimal equivalents.
  - Grade 8 B1.4: Uses fractions, decimals and percents interchangeably, including percents over 100% and under 1%.
- `rounding` — Rounding decimal numbers
  - Grade 6 B1.5: Rounds terminating decimals and repeating decimals (shown with bar notation and written out, e.g. 0.666…) to the nearest whole number, tenth or hundredth, including sharing, measurement and money contexts, and recognising which number rounds to a given value.
  - Grade 7 B1.6: Rounds decimal numbers to the nearest whole number, tenth or hundredth in measurement, money and sharing contexts, including cases that carry (9.996 → 10.00) and averages or unit prices that are rounded at the end.
- `int-addsub` — Adding and subtracting integers
  - Grade 7 B2.4: Adds and subtracts integers and solves temperature, elevation and game-score situations with them. Number lines and zero pairs are shown in prompts, explanations and solutions; representing with objects or diagrams is not itself assessed, only the calculation.
  - Grade 8 B2.4: Adds and subtracts integers, including subtracting negatives and expressions with several terms, in temperature, elevation and score contexts.
- `int-muldiv` — Multiplying and dividing integers
  - Grade 8 B2.7: Multiplies and divides integers: sign rules (recognised and applied), products and quotients, chains of several factors, and temperature, diving and averaging contexts.
- `order-ops` — Order of operations
  - Grade 7 B2.1: Uses the order of operations (BEDMAS) in multi-step calculations with whole numbers, decimal numbers and fractions, including money problems that need brackets. The ratio, rate and percent parts of this expectation are practised in the ratio packs, and naming the properties of operations is explained but not assessed.
  - Grade 8 B2.1: Uses the order of operations in multi-step calculations with integers and other rational numbers (negative fractions and decimals, powers of negative numbers, a mean temperature). Ratio, rate and percent problems are covered by the ratio packs.
- `squares-roots` — Perfect squares and square roots
  - Grade 7 B1.2: Identifies perfect squares, squares whole numbers and finds square roots of perfect squares, including the side length of a square from its area. Square arrays appear in explanations.
  - Grade 8 B2.2: Recalls common square numbers and their square roots, including perfect squares ending in pairs of zeros (900, 4900, 90 000).
  - Grade 8 B1.3: Estimates square roots of non-perfect squares between two consecutive whole numbers, calculates them to the nearest tenth with a calculator (as instructed in the question), and compares a square root with a decimal by squaring.
- `exponents` — Powers and exponents
  - Grade 7 B2.7: Writes repeated multiplication as a power and evaluates powers of whole numbers, including doubling, chain-message and volume contexts, sums and products of powers, and finding an unknown exponent.
  - Grade 7 B1.1: Covers only the expanded-form-with-powers-of-ten part: converting between standard form and expanded form using powers of ten for whole numbers up to one billion. Comparing numbers and describing everyday uses are not assessed here.
- `exponent-laws` — Exponent laws
  - MTH1W B2.2: Uses the product, quotient and power-of-a-power relationships to simplify numeric powers and algebraic monomials (one or two variables, with coefficients), including zero and negative exponents at the stretch level. The patterns behind the laws are shown in explanations; answers are checked as simplified expressions.
- `sci-notation` — Scientific notation
  - Grade 8 B1.1: Writes very large and very small numbers in scientific notation and in standard form, and compares numbers written in scientific notation. Describing everyday uses appears in explanations and contexts but is not assessed.
  - MTH1W B2.1: Evaluates powers with zero and negative exponents (using the pattern of dividing by the base), and expresses and compares numbers in scientific notation, including products and quotients of numbers in scientific notation at the stretch level.
- `divisibility` — Divisibility rules
  - Grade 6 B2.2: Applies the divisibility rules for 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10 to decide whether a number is divisible, to pick a divisible number, to find a missing digit, and to count which of these divisors a number has.
- `prime-factor` — Prime factorization
  - Grade 6 B2.6: Writes composite numbers as a product of prime factors (factor trees are shown in the worked solutions), recognises prime numbers, finds a missing prime factor, picks the correct prime factorization and reasons about the largest prime factor and the number of prime factors.
- `gcf-lcm` — Greatest common factor and lowest common multiple
  - Grade 7 B2.6: Finds the greatest common factor of two or three whole numbers up to 144 and the lowest common multiple of two and three whole numbers, including sharing (kits, rows) and lining-up (packages, bus schedules) problems.
- `percent-of` — Mental math with percents
  - Grade 6 B2.3: Finds 1%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 25%, 50% (and 100%) of whole numbers with mental math strategies, including choosing a correct strategy (15% = 10% + 5%, 5% = half of 10%) and judging claims. Stretch questions build other percents from these pieces or work back from a percent to the whole. Explaining strategies aloud is modelled in the solutions, not assessed.
- `percent-change` — Percent increase and decrease
  - Grade 7 B2.3: Increases and decreases whole numbers by 1%, 5%, 10%, 25%, 50% and 100% with mental math (find the percent, then add or subtract), in plain and everyday contexts (sales, price rises, population growth), including choosing the matching multiplier and judging claims. Stretch questions apply two changes in a row. Explaining the strategy is modelled in solutions, not assessed.
- `proportion` — Ratios, rates and proportions
  - Grade 6 B2.12: Solves ratio problems: equivalent ratios and missing values (with ratio tables in the solutions), unit rates, writing a part-to-whole ratio as a percent, and comparing unit prices to find the better buy. Choosing tools is shown in the lesson, not assessed.
  - Grade 7 B2.10: Identifies proportional and non-proportional situations from tables (true/false: is y ÷ x constant?) and from descriptions (choice: a fee or starting amount means not proportional), and applies proportional reasoning: missing values, unit rates, speed, and sharing in a ratio.
  - Grade 8 B2.8: Compares proportional situations with unit rates (better buy, faster speed), determines unknown values, finds and uses scale factors (including working back to the initial amount), and solves rate problems such as fuel consumption.
  - MTH1W B3.5: Solves problems with rates, percentages and proportions in real-life contexts: unit rates, map scales, flow rates, comparison shopping, sharing in a ratio, and a discount followed by 13% HST. Posing problems is not assessed.
- `exchange-rate` — Exchange rates
  - Grade 7 F1.1: Converts Canadian dollars to foreign currencies and back using a given, clearly labelled practice rate (written either way round), rounding to the cent; compares exchange rates and prices in different currencies, and handles a flat fee and a round trip. Rates are made up for practice; finding current rates is discussed but not assessed.
- `interest` — Simple and compound interest
  - Grade 8 F1.4: Calculates simple interest (I = Prt) and compound interest compounded annually over 1–4 years, rounded to the cent at the end, compares the two, and judges statements about how each grows over time. The expectation mentions digital tools: a spreadsheet-style calculation is shown in the lesson, but the calculation is practised directly. Explaining long-term planning is discussed, not assessed.
  - MTH1W F1.3: Compares total costs of borrowing: simple and annually compounded loans, plans with different down payments, rates and times (total cost = down payment + amount borrowed + interest), and options at different rates. Other compounding periods and tools are mentioned, not assessed.
- `probability` — Probability
  - Grade 6 D2.1: Expresses the probability of an event as a fraction, decimal and percent, places it on the probability line (impossible to certain), predicts results over many trials, and chooses the better option in a game. Drawing a probability line is shown, not assessed.
  - Grade 6 D2.2: Finds theoretical probabilities of two independent events (die, coin, spinner, two dice sums, draws with replacement) by multiplying and by listing outcomes, finds experimental probability from given trial counts, and compares the two. Running the experiment is described, not assessed.
  - Grade 7 D2.2: Finds theoretical probabilities of two independent events and two dependent events (drawing without replacement, including “same colour” and “one of each”), finds experimental probability from given counts, and compares experimental with theoretical results.
  - Grade 8 D2.2: Finds theoretical probabilities of multiple (three) independent events and multiple dependent events (up to three draws without replacement), uses “at least one” with the complement, and compares experimental with theoretical probability from given counts.
- `eval-expr` — Evaluating algebraic expressions
  - Grade 6 C2.2: Evaluates expressions and formulas (perimeter, area, cost) by substituting whole numbers and decimal tenths and following the order of operations.
  - Grade 7 C2.2: Evaluates expressions and formulas (perimeter, area of triangles and trapezoids, volume of prisms) with whole numbers and decimal numbers, including expressions with a squared term.
  - Grade 8 C2.2: Evaluates expressions and formulas with rational numbers: negative integers, fractions and decimals, including squared terms. Expressions with π are not included.
- `like-terms` — Like terms and simplifying expressions
  - Grade 6 C2.1: Adds monomials with a degree of 1 and whole-number coefficients, including in a pattern-block cost context. Tools such as algebra tiles appear in explanations; using them is not assessed.
  - Grade 7 C2.1: Adds and subtracts monomials with a degree of 1 and whole numbers, including finding a missing addend and a word context. Using tools is shown, not assessed.
  - Grade 8 C2.1: Adds and subtracts monomials with integer coefficients (including subtracting negatives) and adds binomials with a degree of 1. Using tools is shown, not assessed.
  - MTH1W C1.4: Simplifies expressions using the distributive property, subtracting binomials, collecting degree 1 and 2 terms, and multiplying monomials. Representations such as area models are explained but not assessed.
  - MTH1W C1.3: Identifies equivalent expressions by simplifying or substituting values (multiple choice and true/false), with the justification shown in the solution. Concrete and graphical comparisons and expressions with integer exponents are not assessed.
- `solve-linear` — Solving equations
  - Grade 6 C2.3: Solves equations with multiple terms and whole numbers (collecting like terms, two-step equations, dividing by a number), including word contexts; every solution shows a substitution check.
  - Grade 7 C2.3: Solves equations with multiple terms, whole numbers and decimal numbers, including a money context and variables on both sides as a stretch; solutions are verified by substitution.
  - Grade 8 C2.3: Solves equations with multiple terms, integers and decimal numbers, including variables on both sides and a temperature context; solutions are verified by substitution.
  - MTH1W C1.5: Creates and solves linear equations: brackets, fractions, proportions, variables on both sides, formulas such as I = Prt, and equations built from contexts (including choosing the equation that models a situation). Degree 2 and 3 equations are not covered.
- `inequalities` — Solving inequalities
  - Grade 6 C2.4: Solves two-operation inequalities with whole numbers up to 100, chooses the solution, finds the least or greatest whole number that works, and verifies values by substitution. Graphing on a number line is explained but not assessed.
  - Grade 7 C2.4: Solves inequalities with multiple terms and whole numbers (including the variable on the right side) and verifies solutions by substitution. Graphing on a number line is explained but not assessed.
  - Grade 8 C2.4: Solves inequalities with integers, including variables on both sides and reversing the sign when dividing by a negative, and verifies solutions. Graphing on a number line is explained but not assessed.
- `pattern-rule` — Pattern rules for linear patterns
  - Grade 6 C1.3: Uses linear growing patterns in tables: near and far predictions, missing terms, writing the rule as an expression and solving for the position of a value. Repeating and shrinking patterns and extending pictures are not covered.
  - Grade 7 C1.3: Uses linear growing patterns with whole numbers and decimal numbers: predictions, missing terms, rules as expressions, solving for unknown positions and extending back to position 0. Repeating patterns are not covered.
  - Grade 8 C1.3: Uses linear growing and shrinking patterns with integers and decimals: predictions, missing terms, rules as expressions, solving for unknowns, and extending to position 0 or negative positions. Graphs of patterns are not assessed.
- `linear-relations` — Linear relations: rate of change and initial value
  - MTH1W C3.2: Connects tables of values, contexts and equations of linear relations: finds the rate of change and initial value from a table or a context and writes the equation. Concrete materials and drawing graphs are explained but not assessed.
  - MTH1W C4.4: Determines slope and y-intercept from a graph, a table or two points, writes the equation y = mx + b, and uses equations to solve problems. Sketching graphs and concrete representations are not assessed.
- `code-trace` — Reading code to predict its output
  - Grade 6 C3.2: Reads short Python programs with variables, arithmetic, if/else and loops and predicts their output. Altering code and describing effects on efficiency are not assessed.
  - Grade 7 C3.2: Reads Python programs with loops that run a defined number of times, while loops, conditions and a simple function (subprogram) and predicts their output. Altering code and describing efficiency are not assessed.
  - Grade 8 C3.2: Reads Python programs that analyse a list of data (total, mean, maximum, counting values above a threshold) and predicts their output. Altering code to inform decisions and describing efficiency are not assessed.
  - MTH1W C2.3: Reads code to predict its outcome: loops that build sums and tables of values for linear relations, while loops and conditions. Altering code to adjust constraints, parameters and outcomes is not assessed.
- `pythagoras` — The Pythagorean theorem
  - Grade 8 E2.4: Applies the Pythagorean theorem to find an unknown hypotenuse or leg of a right triangle: Pythagorean triples give exact answers at the lower levels, other lengths are rounded to the nearest tenth, and stretch questions take two steps. Warm-ups use the areas of the squares on the sides; describing the relationship with physical or geometric models is explained in the lesson but not assessed.
  - MTH1W E1.5: Solves right-triangle side-length problems in real-life situations (ladders, ramps, screens, canoe routes, checking that a corner is square) and in composite shapes (a shed front with a triangular roof, the diagonal of a box), rounding to the nearest tenth.
- `circles` — Circles: circumference and area
  - Grade 7 E2.3: Uses d = 2r and C = πd (or C = 2πr) to find a circumference from a radius or diameter, including in word problems, and works backwards from a circumference to the diameter or radius. Measuring real objects with string to discover π is described in the lesson but not assessed.
  - Grade 7 E2.5: Uses A = πr² to find the area of a circle from its radius or diameter, including half circles, and works backwards from an area to the radius or diameter. The cut-and-rearrange argument that explains the formula is shown in the lesson but not assessed.
- `volume` — Volume of prisms, cylinders, pyramids and cones
  - Grade 7 E2.7: Finds the volume of rectangular prisms, triangular prisms and cylinders as area of the base × height, and finds the base area or the height when the other two measurements are given. Building prisms from layers of cubes is shown in the lesson but not assessed.
  - MTH1W E1.6: Uses the relationship that a pyramid or cone holds one third of the prism or cylinder with the same base and height to find volumes and missing heights, including answers in mL and L. Surface area is not covered here.
- `angles` — Angle properties
  - Grade 6 E2.3: Finds unknown angles using complementary (90°), supplementary (180°) and opposite angles, the 180° sum of a triangle’s interior angles, angles on a line or around a point, and exterior angles of a triangle. Measuring with a protractor is not assessed.
  - Grade 8 E2.2: Solves for unknown angles with intersecting lines and with parallel lines crossed by a transversal (alternate, corresponding and co-interior angles), using the (n − 2) × 180° sum of a polygon’s interior angles, and the interior and exterior angles of regular polygons. Writing formal proofs is not assessed.
- `area-shapes` — Areas of trapezoids, rhombuses, kites and composite shapes
  - Grade 6 E2.4: Finds the areas of trapezoids, rhombuses, kites and composite polygons by decomposing them into rectangles, parallelograms and triangles (or with the formulas those decompositions give), and works backwards to a missing height or diagonal. Drawing the decomposition is modelled in the lesson; only the resulting area is assessed.
- `unit-convert` — Metric units: converting length, mass, capacity, area and volume
  - Grade 6 E2.1: Converts lengths, masses and capacities between metric units (mm, cm, m, km; mg, g, kg; mL, L) in both directions, and solves problems that need a conversion first, such as comparing two containers. Choosing tools and measuring physical objects is not assessed.
  - Grade 7 E2.1: Applies 1 mL = 1 cm³ (and 1 L = 1000 cm³) to find the capacity of containers from their inside measurements, and distinguishes volume (space taken up) from capacity (what a container holds). Describing the difference in writing is explained in the lesson; it is checked only through multiple-choice and true/false items.
  - Grade 7 E2.2: Solves perimeter, area and volume problems that need a unit conversion, including cm² ↔ m² (× 10 000), mm² ↔ cm² (× 100) and cm³ ↔ m³ (× 1 000 000), and measurements given in mixed units.
- `coordinates` — The coordinate plane and transformations
  - Grade 6 E1.3: Reads the coordinates of points in all four quadrants, names quadrants, finds the image of a point after a translation, describes the translation that moves one point to another, and works backwards from an image. Plotting points by hand is shown in diagrams but not assessed.
  - Grade 7 E1.4: Predicts the coordinates of the image of a point after a translation, a reflection in the x- or y-axis, a rotation of 90° or 180° about the origin, or two of these in a row. Drawing the transformed shapes is not assessed, and rotations about centres other than the origin are not covered.

## How this is verified

- `npm run curriculum:verify` downloads the live Ministry data again and compares it with the shipped bundle record by record (added, removed or reworded expectations and courses). It exits non-zero on any difference.
- In the app, Student Learning → Coverage → "Check the Ministry site for changes" does the same comparison and only applies changes when asked, keeping the earlier wording in the revision history.
- Curriculum files imported by hand are always marked unverified.
- `tests/unit/content.test.ts` checks that every skill-pack mapping points at a real expectation and that every generated answer key passes an independent recomputation.
